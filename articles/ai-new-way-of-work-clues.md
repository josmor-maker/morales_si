# A Clue About How AI Is Changing Human Roles

*One Conversation with an AI Engineer*

Just a few days ago, at a party with friends, we started talking about AI and how our jobs are changing. One of my friends started describing a hiring process he is going through. Pretty amazing...
Subscribe now
He is applying for a role as an AI Engineer. After many questions and back and forth, he finally gets more details on what the role actually looks like. And what he described was surprising — let me paint it for you.
He receives a ticket with specifications. That is the input. Then he uses a set of AI agents, tools, and workflows to process that input and produce an outcome. If the ticket requires it, he creates new tools and processes, and then lets the system do the work.
He asked about interactions with team members. The answer was essentially: you work independently, with minimal interaction. Your role is not only engineering — it is also product management. You and your agents are a mini team.
Wow. That blew my mind.

And apparently, this is already being named. Nate B. Jones, AI strategist and former Head of Product at Amazon Prime Video, recently wrote about exactly this emerging role:

“It isn’t GTM Engineers. It’s Deployment Strategists. Decagon calls it an ‘Agent Product Manager.’ Harvey calls it a ‘Solutions Architect.’ And the salaries are climbing fast.” — Nate B. Jones, LinkedIn (https://www.linkedin.com/in/natebjones/)
I can easily see companies hiring AI orchestrators to control most information flows in a business. The AI engineer needs to think like a product manager, plan like a project manager, and execute like an engineer. Not only in software creation, but on anything. The human is in charge of judgment and tooling. AI processes the information. The human assumes the responsibility.
