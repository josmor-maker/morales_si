# AI, LLMs, Open Source and Digital Sovereignty

*Jenny made my homework but I added something else :)*

TL;DR: Jenny Ouyang mapped what "open source" actually means in AI right now: most of it is open-weight, not open source, and the license is what can quietly bite you later. I add a layer: even the compliant models are sovereign to different degrees. I run today's open models through four questions: can you see the training data, run it without permission, keep that permission, and survive the vendor disappearing?

I had an idea sitting in my drafts: write something about open-source LLMs, their licenses, and what sovereignty actually means when the model underneath you isn’t yours.

Then published a field guide to open-source LLMs that did my homework better than I ever would have.
So instead of writing my version, here’s hers, plus the piece I still think is missing.

Her argument, stripped down: almost nothing labeled “open” in AI actually is. A license, a business strategy, and a hosting bill all borrow the same word, and nobody separates them for you. Meta gives Llama away not out of generosity but to keep a rival’s paid product from being worth paying for. Then the same weapon changed hands — Chinese labs picked it up and aimed it at everyone, including Meta. She walks through which licenses will actually let you build a business (Apache 2.0, mostly), which ones have a ceiling you’ll hit without noticing (Llama, Qwen), and where the same model can cost four different prices depending on which GPUs it’s running on. It’s the clearest map of that layer I’ve read.
