# I Was the Fly on the Wall When Bill Gates and Ezra Klein Talked AI

*But they couldn't hear me! So I'm telling you instead.*

*What I would have told Bill Gates and Ezra Klein, if anyone had been listening*

I finished watching Ezra Klein’s long interview with Bill Gates on a Saturday morning, coffee gone cold beside the keyboard. Gates had just published an essay saying the AI transition will be one of the most turbulent periods in human history, and that nobody is preparing for it. For an hour and a quarter, the two of them circled the question of what happens next.

When it ended, I closed my eyes and made a wish that I suspect many of you have made after a great conversation you were not invited to:

***I would love to have been a fly on the wall.***

![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/59f1b246-f136-4e1f-abb3-b119eff1153f_1200x675.gif)

So I dreamed it. What follows is that dream. Gates and Klein say what they actually said, paraphrased, because the auto-captions were too rough to quote. The fly is me, although I do not understand the symbolism yet... :)

## Thirty years of being wrong

Klein opens with a clip from 1996. A young Gates on Letterman, explaining that making computers think is a very hard problem, that almost no progress has been made, that some people believe it will never happen. Letterman jokes that we wouldn’t want them to think anyway. Gates agrees: a scary thought.
