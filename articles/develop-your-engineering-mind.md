# Develop Your Engineering Mind

*We all have one — nourish it, and stay relevant in the age of AI*

In my last post, I playfully suggested that “Vibe Coding is both sides of the Force,” hinting that skepticism and curiosity should mix if we want to get the best out of AI.
This time, I’d like to take a different angle: why it’s essential to develop your Engineering Mind.

Have you ever heard that “having a personal philosophy” is important for navigating life’s ups and downs? In the same way, when new technologies arrive, we need a point of view. One that considers what’s available today, a short-term vision, and a long-term vision.
But philosophy alone is not enough. Action and integration are essential.

Suggested Reading
📖 “Vibe coding has turned senior devs into ‘AI babysitters,’ but they say it’s worth it” (TechCrunch)
Senior developers experimenting with vibe coding discovered it doesn’t replace their work — it complicates it. But instead of resisting, they learned to integrate it. Vibe coding is fast and messy, yet it still makes their jobs better. The fear of “replacement” turned into practical curiosity.
📖 “Public Intelligence” (Kevin Kelly)
Kelly imagines a future AI commons: open, federated, and global — a “public utility of intelligence” shaped by everyone. He warns that focusing only on ROI and short-term gains is not enough; if we disengage, others will decide the trajectory of AI, just as happened with social media.
Together, these perspectives show us why we need to cultivate an Engineering Mind: curiosity to explore what works today, and vision to participate in shaping the future.
