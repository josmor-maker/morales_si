# Here you have it, a prompt that builds your own content research tool.

*The Substack Post Analyzer, featured in Cash & Cache, comes with the full prompt so you can adapt it to your workflow and your tools.*

TL;DR — I built a Chrome Extension called the Substack Post Analyzer that reads articles, matches quotes to my personal themes, and lets me store or publish them instantly. It was recently featured in 's roundup of real Cowork workflows. In this article, I show you how it works, why I built it as a Chrome Extension, what you need to get started, and — most importantly — I include the full prompt that builds the tool for you, customized to your themes, your database, and your workflow. Built for Claude Cowork, but adaptable to your preferred AI tool.

A few days ago, Cash & Cache featured my Substack Post Analyzer in their roundup of Cowork workflows, and I want to use that as an opportunity to show you how it works in more detail. But more than that, I want you to walk away with ideas for building something similar that fits your own workflow.
The tool was born out of frustration. Every time I found a quote worth sharing while reading Substack, I had to go through the same painful sequence: read, copy, paste, tag, decide. Multiply that by a reading session with five or six good articles, and it becomes a real drag. I wanted a way to capture quotes that matched my specific themes — not just anything that sounded good, but content that aligned with what I actually write about.
