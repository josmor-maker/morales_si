# The Two Sides of AI Everyone Should Know About

*Predictions that are already happening — and the use of AI few are talking about*

TL;DR: Two stories came out this week that, together, tell a bigger story. One shows you what AI is doing to affect the way we work. The other shows what AI is being trained to do. Here’s what I see when I put them side by side.

This week, I cancelled my ChatGPT subscription. I’ve been a daily user for a long time — it was woven into my workflows and automations. (I’ll share a practical article soon on how I migrated my automations from ChatGPT to Claude — stay tuned.) But something happened that made me choose a side. Let me show you both sides of the story.

The AI Adoption Map
Anthropic published new research measuring AI’s real impact on jobs, not what it could theoretically do, but what people are actually using it for at work, right now.
Computer programmers. Customer service representatives. Data entry workers. Financial analysts. These are the roles with the highest real-world AI exposure today.

The direction is clear: jobs with the highest exposure are already projected to grow less over the next decade. And there is early evidence that hiring of young workers, 22 to 25 year olds, is already slowing in those same fields.
I wrote about this in January: the learning ladder is disappearing. The entry-level work that used to build domain expertise is being eliminated before the next generation can use it. The data is now confirming it.
Here’s the key insight: this is not just a trend. It’s a map. It shows where pressure is building right now, before it becomes visible in unemployment numbers.
