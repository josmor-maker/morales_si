# AI 2026: the challenge is organizational

*Would you work at a company that ignores AI?*

AI is not really challenging people. It’s more challenging for organizations.
What AI is exposing is not a lack of talent, but a lack of organizational imagination:

Rigid processes, slow decision-making, and ineffective structures.
Adapting late is a choice many businesses will make. But the cost compounds:

Inefficiency in comparison with competitors.

Frustration and friction caused by a lack of expertise.

Loss of talent.

and probably many more.
Adapting early is also risky. Because it forces uncomfortable questions about how work is designed, who decides, and what actually matters.
The real opportunity isn’t replacing jobs. It’s redesigning work.

AI creates space for:

Smaller, more effective teams

Closer relationships with people and customers

More humane organizations
Some roles will disappear. That’s true. But they make room for something more interesting.
New functions that don’t fit old job titles yet:

People who shape context and judgment

People who continuously adapt processes

People who decide what shouldn’t be automated
And for many, this opens another door:
Working independently, outside the corporate system, building small, meaningful, resilient businesses.
The question isn’t “Will AI take my job?”
It’s “Is my workplace actively rethinking how it evolves using AI?”
Because people will adapt. The real uncertainty is whether organizations will.
I’m curious:
Are we already designing these new roles and organizations?
Happy 2026!
