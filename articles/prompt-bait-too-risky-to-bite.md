# Prompt-bait: too risky to bite?

*Are prompt formulas a better approach?*

This is an incomplete judgment, and probably an unpopular post. But I want to share it with you, to get your criticism and build from that. Deal?

Over the past months, I’ve noticed a growing pattern around AI: prompts, bundles of prompts, and small “systems” being shared or sold with the promise of saving people a lot of time. Some of them are genuinely clever. Some are well-intentioned experiments. But do they really save you time? Is it possible that they are actually creating a problem for you?
Something about this trend makes me uneasy.
I’ve started calling it prompt-bait.
It is not an accusation, but a way to name a risk.
Subscribe now

What I mean by prompt-bait
Prompt-bait is not “sharing prompts” per se. Sharing prompts can be useful, generous, and even educational.
Prompt-bait appears when the artifact (a prompt, a workflow, a small automation) is presented as the solution, while the context, assumptions, and inevitable customization work remain unclear.
The promise is usually implicit:

“Use this and you’ll save tons of time.”
“Use this and you’ll be X times more productive.”
What’s rarely made explicit is:

that the prompt will break,

that it will need adjustment,

that it depends on a workflow you may not yet have,
