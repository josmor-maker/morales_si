# I Asked 17 AI Experts What Matters for Businesses. Here's What They Said.

*Bridging Business and IT for AI Strategic Implementation*

When I shared the survey with , a friend who has spent over 25 years inside IT, now a strategic advisor for IT leaders navigating AI-era transformation, she offered something very useful. She offered a perspective.
In her experience, IT is not always seen as a strategic partner in a company. Often it functions as a service layer: responding to needs, managing infrastructure, staying out of the boardroom conversation. She pointed out that real AI adoption happens at the user level — behavior, language, process — and that for it to work, IT and business need to speak to each other, not past each other.
She was describing exactly the gap this survey is trying to address.

What the research says
The tension between IT and business around AI is not an exception. It is the norm.
A 2025 survey by Writer and Workplace Intelligence found that 68% of executives report that AI has created tension between IT teams and other business areas, and 72% say their company develops AI applications in silos. The McKinsey State of AI 2025 report confirms that while 88% of organizations use AI in at least one function, only about one third have begun to scale — and high performers are three times more likely to have senior leaders who demonstrate active ownership of their AI initiatives. Meanwhile, Deloitte’s 2026 State of AI in the Enterprise reports that 84% of organizations are now factoring Digital Sovereignty into their AI strategies — a signal that the conversation is maturing beyond tools and toward strategic independence.
Most companies are using AI. Few are thinking strategically about it. And even fewer have a real conversation happening between business leadership and IT about what it means for the company’s future.
